
# Positioning Brief: Entering the Competitor-Tracking Market

## The landscape today

Three vendors dominate the space, and they all look more alike than different once you get past the marketing:

- **Crayon** positions itself as the broad platform: track many competitors in depth, not just battlecards, with an AI assistant (Crayon Answers) and AI review analysis on top. It has strong reviews (G2 4.6/5 from 385 reviews) and is genuinely easy to set up and admin (9.1 and 9.2 out of 10 on G2). But it has a recurring, specific complaint: the AI deduplication and relevance filtering isn't good enough, so feeds get cluttered and users end up manually sorting through noise.

- **Klue** positions itself as the sales-enablement-first alternative to Crayon: "Klue is much more focused on enabled competitive intelligence and enabling the sales+ teams, while Crayon was more focused on enabling marketing." Its Salesforce integration is a genuine standout, surfacing the right battlecard at the right deal stage. But its Boards feature is less intuitive and harder to organize intelligence in, and running it well requires dedicated staff time for setup and curation.

- **Kompyte** (now part of Semrush) tracks the widest range of digital signals (site changes, social, reviews, content, job postings) and scores well on effective competitor tracking (9.0 on Competitive Analysis). It leans on the Semrush ecosystem and sells demo-first with dedicated customer success rather than self-serve. It has no public pricing and no lightweight trial path, which is a bigger liability for it than for the others since it's the one most explicitly selling ecosystem lock-in.

All three: quote-based pricing only, no rate card, entry price estimated at $15,000-20,000+ per year (Crayon, Klue), with Klue scaling into low-six-figure annual contracts for 15-50 user deployments. All three sell top-down into enterprise CI, product marketing, and sales enablement teams, generally demo-first, sales-led cycles.

The category logic behind all of them is sound: 65% of B2B software sales opportunities are competitive, and 78% of CI leaders already enable sales with battlecards, so there's demonstrated demand for this category, not a demand problem.

## Where the gaps are

**1. Opaque pricing is a category-wide weakness, not just a Crayon or Klue problem.** No vendor publishes rates. Everyone requires a sales call to find out what it costs. For a mid-market or SMB buyer trying to build a business case quickly, this is friction that has nothing to do with product quality, and it's an opening any vendor willing to publish tiered, transparent pricing can walk through.

**2. Feed noise is a real, named pain point, not a hypothetical one.** Crayon's own users complain about deduplication and relevance filtering falling short, and Kompyte's core selling point is AI filtering to cut through noise, which implies the market broadly experiences this as unsolved. Anyone entering the category should treat clean signal (not more sources, not more AI features, just fewer wrong alerts) as a core product requirement, not an add-on.

**3. Setup and ongoing curation still require real human time.** Crayon is the exception on ease-of-setup, but Klue explicitly needs dedicated staff to curate and organize intelligence well. That's a fine trade-off for an enterprise CI team with headcount, but it rules these tools out for smaller teams who want something closer to "set it up in an afternoon and mostly leave it alone."

**4. Nobody is serving mid-market/SMB on its own terms.** Pricing structures assume 15-50+ seat deployments (Klue) or custom enterprise quotes (Crayon, Kompyte). None offer a genuine self-serve or low-touch entry point. Klue "skews mid-market/enterprise" per G2 data but that's still the smaller-company end of an otherwise enterprise-first field, not a product built for SMB from the ground up. A team of 2-10 people in product marketing or sales at a smaller company currently has no credible option in this category that isn't overkill and overpriced for them.

## Recommended positioning

Position as **the competitor-tracking tool for teams without a dedicated CI function**: mid-market and SMB product marketing, sales, and founder-led teams who need to know what competitors are doing without hiring a CI analyst or signing a low-six-figure contract.

This is a direct contrast to all three incumbents, who are built for CI teams with headcount and enterprise budgets. It does not compete with Crayon on breadth of AI features, Klue on Salesforce depth, or Kompyte on channel coverage. It competes on being usable and affordable by a team that has none of those things.

## Messaging angle

The one thing worth saying: **"Know what your competitors are doing without hiring someone to watch them."**

Supporting points, each tied to a real gap:
- **Published pricing, not a demo call.** Every competitor requires a sales conversation to learn the price. Say the number up front.
- **Fewer, better alerts.** The named failure mode of the category leader (Crayon) is noisy feeds needing manual sorting. Make "we tell you less, but it's the right less" the product promise, not a footnote.
- **Runs itself.** Klue needs dedicated curation staff to work well. Position as the tool that doesn't require a part-time analyst to keep useful.
- **Built for the team that has one person doing this, not five.** Not "enterprise CI, simplified," but genuinely scoped and priced for a small team from day one, not a scaled-down enterprise SKU.

Avoid: competing on AI feature count (Crayon's Answers, Kompyte's channel breadth), competing on Salesforce-native depth (Klue owns that), or trying to sound bigger/more enterprise than the buyer is. The credible wedge is smaller, cheaper, clearer, not "also does everything they do."
